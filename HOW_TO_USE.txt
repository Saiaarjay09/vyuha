WEBSITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  https://saiaarjay09.github.io/vyuha/

  Open it in any browser, on a phone or a computer. Nothing to install, no
  account, and nothing to pay. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, so it is always
  up - it does not depend on anyone's computer being switched on.

  WHAT THE WEBSITE CAN DO

  Plan a goal, project an investment, and check how risky your holdings are.
  All three run inside your own browser: the numbers you type are never sent
  anywhere. It also shows today's market figures and the council's most recent
  verdicts, both refreshed automatically every weekday.

  WHAT IT CANNOT DO

  Answer a NEW question. The council is ten AI advisors, and that needs a
  language model running at the moment you ask - which a free static site
  cannot provide. It shows the latest recorded verdicts instead, and says so.

  To ask your own questions you run Vyuha yourself; see RUNNING IT YOURSELF
  below.

  Note: this is free, open-source software, not a commercial service. Nobody
  is monitoring it and nothing is guaranteed.


WHAT IT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Ask a question about markets - in India or anywhere in the world - in plain
  English, and get an answer grounded in live data.

  Indian markets are covered in the most depth: the RBI, the NSE, options,
  institutional flows. Beyond that: gold and silver at the London fix, crude,
  metals and grains; the S&P 500, Nasdaq and world interest rates; 166
  currencies; and economic data for 217 countries.

  IMPORTANT FOR AN INDIAN INVESTOR: foreign prices are shown in rupees as well
  as dollars. Your return on a foreign asset is the asset's move AND the
  rupee's move. Over the year to September 2026 the S&P 500 rose 15.2% in
  dollars, but the rupee weakened, so an Indian investor received 25.4% - the
  currency was a THIRD of the return. Tools that quote only the dollar figure
  are telling you the wrong number.

  Behind the page, ten AI advisors each look at the same real figures - pulled
  live from the Reserve Bank of India and the National Stock Exchange - and
  each gives its own estimate. Every advisor is deliberately given a DIFFERENT
  outlook: one worries about inflation, another about weak growth, one thinks
  shares are overpriced, another follows the trend, and one is there purely to
  argue against whatever the others conclude.

  Their answers are then combined into a single number.

  Why bother with ten instead of one? Because a single opinion hides its own
  blind spots. Ten opinions that disagree show you where the uncertainty
  actually is. The disagreement is part of the answer, not noise to hide.


HOW TO USE IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Type a question and press Ask. Three kinds of question work:

  1. "WILL SOMETHING HAPPEN?" - you get a probability.

       Will the Nifty 50 fall below 22,900 in the next 30 days?
       Will the RBI cut rates before December?
       Will gold cross 5,000 dollars an ounce this year?
       Should an Indian investor expect US stocks to beat the Nifty?

     Best results come from questions with a clear yes/no answer and a
     timeframe. "Will the market go up?" is too vague to answer well.

  2. "WHAT IS X RIGHT NOW?" - you get the live figure, with no AI involved
     at all. Nothing is estimated or guessed; it is read straight from the
     source.

       What is the repo rate right now?
       What is the price of gold?
       Where is the S&P 500 trading?
       What is the dollar worth in rupees?

  3. "WHAT IF THINGS GO WRONG?" - you get losses an example portfolio would
     have taken in real past crises.

       What happens to a portfolio in a market crash?
       Stress test against a taper tantrum


HOW LONG IT TAKES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  A figure lookup ("what is the repo rate") is near-instant.

  A forecast question takes roughly 20-25 seconds, because ten advisors each
  read the data and answer, and then deliberate a second time if they
  disagreed. A progress line tells you how many have replied.

  If they agree in the first round, the second is skipped automatically -
  there is nothing to deliberate about - and the answer arrives sooner.


READING THE ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The answer opens with a verdict - "Unlikely", "Too close to call", "Likely" -
  the percentage, and then ONE PARAGRAPH that tells you the whole thing: how
  confident the council is, how much the advisors disagreed, the range of their
  estimates, who dissented most strongly, and which figures they used.

  If you only read one thing, read that paragraph.

  Below it, four numbered sections hold the detail:

    01  The advisors     every estimate and the reasoning behind it
    02  The case against the strongest argument the other way
    03  The evidence     every figure they were given
    04  Method           the technical numbers

  The paragraph also tells you whether the advisors agreed:

    "agreed almost entirely"    the estimate is reasonably firm
    "mostly agreed"             a few leaned differently, no real split
    "disagreed noticeably"      treat it as a rough guide
    "disagreed sharply"         there is no reliable answer here

  That last one is not a failure. If ten advisors looking at the same data
  reach opposite conclusions, the honest output is "nobody knows", and the
  page says so rather than inventing false confidence.

  Advisors may ONLY use the figures they are shown. They are not allowed to
  invent numbers, and if one cites a figure it was never given, the system
  flags it.


WHAT IT CANNOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * It cannot predict the market. Nobody can. It estimates how likely
    something is, and it is often wrong.

  * It is not investment advice. It is analytical software. Do not buy or
    sell anything because of a number on this page.

  * It cannot detect tiny movements. There is a hard physical limit here: the
    smallest price change the NSE can even represent is 5 paise. Any claim to
    detect moves finer than that is false, no matter who makes it.

  * Housing and property are NOT covered at all, and it will tell you so
    rather than guess. Per-country share prices outside the US are also thin.

  * Its data has gaps. Some Indian government sources publish weeks late,
    revise their numbers afterwards, or have stopped publishing altogether.
    The system records what it does not have rather than filling holes with
    guesses.

  * Right now all ten advisors run on the same AI model, so they tend to make
    the same mistakes. The page warns you when this is the case. Installing
    more models fixes it.


RUNNING IT YOURSELF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Everything is open source under the Apache 2.0 licence.

    git clone git@github.com:Saiaarjay09/vyuha.git
    cd vyuha
    python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
    pip install -e ".[api,dev]"
    ./scripts/serve.sh

  Then open http://127.0.0.1:8601

  For the AI advisors you also need Ollama (https://ollama.com). More model
  families means genuinely independent advisors:

    ollama pull qwen2.5:32b
    ollama pull llama3.1:70b
    ollama pull deepseek-r1
    ollama pull gemma2:27b

  Check everything is wired up:

    vyuha doctor
